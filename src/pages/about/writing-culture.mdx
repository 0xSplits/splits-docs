# Writing culture

## Why?

Thinking is hard. Learning is hard. Communicating is hard. Coordinating is hard. Building institutional knowledge is hard. Writing won't make these things easy, but it will stop us from fooling ourselves into avoiding them because they are hard.

Writing is thinking: writing forces us to create coherent narratives out of the jumble of facts and beliefs floating around in our heads. This process will reveal and resolve misconceptions and contradictions we didn't know we held, as well as lead to new questions and ideas.

Writing is learning: writing leaves a paper trail to return to in the future. It is easy to convince ourselves in the future that we "knew" something all along, but leaving a paper trail prevents that. Paper trails show future readers where, how, and why our earlier thinking was mistaken. And we can only learn after we recognize our mistakes.

Writing is communicating: writing is an asynchronous, 1-to-N form of communication. All your present and future teammates get the exact same message.

Writing is coordinating: writing generates a single source of truth. A single source of truth allows everyone to agree on who needs to do what when. We can then understand how our efforts will combine with our teammates' efforts to reach our goal.

Writing generates institutional knowledge: as an organization, we should never be too reliant on a single person. Writing turns one person's knowledge into our organization's knowledge.

Simple & concise writing increases these effects.

## What?

We will document and organize important processes and big decisions.

## How?

We will use the "why / what / how" framework because it is simple and puts the "why" first.

Putting the "why" first grounds the document. We must understand the problem before we can solve it. A compelling problem is also motivating—humans like to solve problems.

Next comes the "what". It should be short, concrete, and actionable.

Last, "how" details our proposed implementation and evaluation. It may be longer and more complicated. Even so, less is still more. Focus through brevity. Power through focus.

This structure begs important questions. Does this "why" matter? Does this "what" help this "why"? Does this "how" do this "what"? When we can answer "yes" to each of these, we are ready to begin.
