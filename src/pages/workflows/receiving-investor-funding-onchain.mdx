# Receiving investor funding onchain

*Last updated 29 October 2025*

A number of folks have used Splits to receive capital from outside investors (VCs, angels, etc). While we intend to bring more of this "in house" over time, here's what we're seeing folks do to make this process easier.

## Verify your investors

Before accepting investment, it is important to complete KYC (Know Your Customer) or KYB (Know Your Business) verification of your investors (NAL — talk to legal counsel).

Today, this can be accomplished through our [Compliance](https://splits.org/changelog/splits-changelog-compliant-payments-live/) flow (currently behind a feature flag, please reach out for access).

![](/images/receiving-investor-funding-onchain/img-1.png)

Select *Invite recipient* and input their email address. Leave the optional *Address* input blank.

![](/images/receiving-investor-funding-onchain/img-2.png)

![](/images/receiving-investor-funding-onchain/img-3.png)

You will be notified by email once the investor has completed KYC or KYB, depending on whether they are an individual or entity.

Stripe also offers government-issued identity [document](https://docs.stripe.com/identity/verification-checks?type=document), email, and [selfie](https://docs.stripe.com/identity/verification-checks?type=selfie) verification, which you may use.

## Create dedicated Accounts

Once you have your Treasury and other operating accounts set up, you can create dedicated accounts for each outside investor. Click the *+* button next to *Accounts* in the nav to create a new account.

![](/images/receiving-investor-funding-onchain/img-4.png)

Decide on a naming pattern for each account you're going to create, that will make it easy to know who the account is for. For example, "Jane Doe $10k". This way, you know to give Jane Doe this account's address, and you can expect them to send their $10k investment.

![](/images/receiving-investor-funding-onchain/img-5.png)

This account will just act as a pass-thru, so select an appropriately safe set of signers. For angel checks, one signer might be sufficient. For larger checks, you may want a comparable amount to your Treasury.

Once you have your Accounts set up, share each account's address with the respective outside investor. As funds start flowing in, you will be able to tell precisely which investors have contributed capital and which ones are still outstanding. Given the finality of USDC transfers, you may want to consider asking the investor to send a single USDC first. Once you confirm receipt to the expected account, they can send the rest confidently.

Once an investor has contributed capital, you can empty that account's balance by forwarding it to your Treasury. It may be helpful to add a memo for each of these transactions, so you can keep a record of the funds moving into your Treasury.

Once the account is empty, click *Settings* in the top right and then scroll down to the *Remove* section and click *Remove from app*. This will "archive" the account from your team, preventing it from showing up in your list of Accounts.
