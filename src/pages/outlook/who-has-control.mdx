# Who has control

We're strong proponents of the [Three Address Protocol](https://x.com/punk6529/status/1701623475725533524). The idea is simple: separate your high-value assets from your day-to-day money. This separation creates natural air gaps, since one account getting compromised doesn't put endanger other accounts.

This system also makes operations easier. [Modern](https://ramp.com/corporate-cards) [fintech](https://www.brex.com/product/credit-card) has figured this out, by letting you spin up accounts and cards for specific purposes (departments, vendors, events, etc).

The ultimate challenge, like in any complex system involving people, is *who ultimately has control*. For a company, it's the shareholders (well, technically, [only those with voting power](https://observer.com/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-2023-shareholder-meeting/)). Most modern web apps have a notion of [roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-based_access_control), which come with different permissions.
